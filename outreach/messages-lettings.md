# Selling compliance to letting agents

Same rules as the trades scripts: two sentences, then a question. The difference
is this buyer is at a desk, is on LinkedIn, and is *scared of one specific thing*.

That thing is **Section 21**. If the prescribed documents are not in place, a
possession claim gets thrown out, and the agent loses a landlord. That is a
business-ending conversation with a client, and it is the entire pitch.

**Never say "AI".** Say "nothing expires without you knowing". It's the same
product described as an outcome.

---

## Who to call

Not the branch negotiator — they don't buy anything. Ask for the **owner** or
**director**, or whoever handles "compliance / property management". In a
5–15 branch agency that is usually one of two people and they are the ones
losing sleep.

Best times: Tuesday–Thursday, 9:30–11:00 or 14:00–16:00. Avoid Monday (deals
day) and Friday.

---

## 1. The email (agencies are Ltd companies, so cold email is allowed)

Subject: `EICRs at [Agency Name] — one question`

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick question, no pitch: if a tenant's gas certificate expired last month,
> how long before anyone at [Agency] knows about it?
>
> I'm building a tracker that answers that automatically across every property
> you manage — gas, EICR, EPC, alarms, deposit deadlines — and tells you 60 days
> out instead of after the fact. It's aimed at [town] agencies with 50–500 properties.
>
> Worth 15 minutes to see if it's relevant?
>
> [Name] · [phone] · [link]
> Reply STOP and I'll leave you alone.

That question lands because they genuinely don't know the answer. Somebody's
tracking it in a spreadsheet that gets updated when someone remembers.

---

## 2. The call

> "Hi — could I speak to whoever looks after compliance, or the owner?
>
> Thanks. I'll be 40 seconds. I'm not selling marketing, I'm asking about
> certificates. When an EICR runs out on a property you manage, how do you
> currently find out — is it a spreadsheet, or does someone remember?
>
> … Right. So how many properties are you managing now? And of those, do you
> know off the top of your head how many certificates expire in the next
> 90 days?"

Then be quiet. The honest answer is "no", and the silence does the selling.

> "I've built the thing that answers that. It's free to try for 30 days on your
> actual portfolio — I'll import your properties myself. If it doesn't catch
> something you'd have missed, don't pay me. Can I show you Thursday?"

---

## 3. The LinkedIn message (they live here)

> Hi [Name] — saw you're running [Agency] in [town]. I've built a compliance
> tracker for lettings portfolios (gas, EICR, EPC, deposits) that flags expiries
> 60 days out instead of after the fact. I'm looking for two [town] agencies to
> trial it free and tell me what's wrong with it. Interested, or should I leave
> you to it?

The "tell me what's wrong with it" framing gets replies from people who'd never
answer a sales message.

---

## 4. Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We use [big property software]" | "Good — then this sits alongside it as the compliance check. Their reports tell you what's expired; this tells you what's about to." |
| "We've got a spreadsheet" | "Everyone has. The question is who opens it, and what happens the month they're on holiday." |
| "Too expensive" | "One avoided Section 21 failure is worth more than a year of it. And the first 30 days are free." |
| "Who are you?" | "Nineteen, building this in [town], no agency behind me. That's why it's £149 and not £1,500 — and why you get me on the phone, not a support queue." |
| "Is my data safe?" | "It stays in your browser, on your machine, for the trial. Nothing leaves unless you ask me to move it to a server." |

That last one matters — be able to say it truthfully. The demo genuinely stores
data locally.

---

## 5. Legal notes for this niche

- **Cold email to a limited company** is permitted under PECR with sender
  identification and a working opt-out. Sole-trader agencies are individual
  subscribers — use LinkedIn or phone for those instead.
- **Check CTPS** before cold-calling a limited company.
- Once you hold real tenant data you are a **data controller**: ICO registration
  (~£50/yr), a lawful basis, and a retention rule. For the trial it stays on
  their machine, which is both the honest answer and the easy one.
- **Never** position this as legal advice. It is a reminder system. The agent
  remains responsible for compliance, and your terms should say so plainly.

---

## 6. What week one looks like

- 40 agencies emailed or messaged → 6–10 replies → **2–3 demos**
- 2–3 demos → **1 paid trial**
- 3 paying agencies at £149/mo = £447/mo, which is your £300/week with room.

Then the number that actually matters: **properties under management.** Ten
agencies at 150 properties is 1,500 doors — that's the metric you raise money on.
