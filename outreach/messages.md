# The messages

The offer page is the easy bit. This is the bit that decides whether you get paid.

Rules that matter more than the wording:

1. **One channel at a time.** Phone or in person beats every DM and email for trades. They are not reading their inbox on a roof.
2. **Two sentences, then a question.** Long messages get deleted. Your goal on the first touch is a reply, not a sale.
3. **Name the specific thing you noticed.** "I called your number at 2pm on Tuesday and got voicemail" is worth more than any amount of clever copy. It is also unarguable.
4. **Never say "AI".** Say what it does. "It texts them back before they ring the next bloke" lands; "AI-powered lead automation" does not.
5. **Offer the free month before they ask about price.** It moves the conversation from "how much" to "does it work".

---

## 1. The first call (best channel for trades)

Ring at 8:00–8:45am or 4:30–5:30pm. Not midday, not Friday afternoon.

> "Morning — is that Dave? I'm not selling you a website, I'll be 30 seconds.
> I'm local, I build the thing that texts customers back when you miss a call.
> Quick question: when your phone rings and you're under a floor, who answers it?"

Wait. Let them talk. If they say "nobody, it goes to voicemail" — that is your opening:

> "Right. So how many do you reckon you miss in a week? … Three or four at £180 a job
> is a couple of grand a month going to whoever answers next.
> I'll set it up on your number, run it free for thirty days, and if it doesn't
> book you at least one job you don't pay anything. Can I show you on Thursday?"

Then **book a specific slot before you hang up.** "Thursday at 4" beats "I'll send you some info".

---

## 2. The walk-in (best for shops, salons, dentists, gyms)

Go in at a quiet time, ask for the owner by name if you can, and have the page open on your phone.

> "Hi — are you the owner? I'll be quick, I know you're busy.
> I noticed you've got no way to book online, so anyone who finds you at 9pm
> just leaves. I fix that. Can I show you on your own phone, takes 90 seconds?"

Show the demo widget. Let *them* type the enquiry and watch the reply land. That single moment sells more than any pitch — they see their own customer being answered.

---

## 3. The email (only for limited companies — see the legal note)

Subject: `missed calls at [Business Name]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I rang [Business Name] yesterday at 2:10pm and it went to voicemail — no text back.
> I'm guessing that happens a lot when you're on a job.
>
> I set up automatic text-backs and booking for [niche] in [town]. First 30 days
> free, no contract, works behind your existing number.
>
> Worth 15 minutes this week?
>
> [Your name] · [phone] · [site]
> Reply STOP and I won't contact you again.

The `STOP` line is not decoration — it is how you stay on the right side of the rules.

---

## 4. Follow-ups (where most of the money is)

Most replies come on touch 2 and 3. Most people quit after touch 1.

**Day 3 — the useful nudge, no guilt:**
> "Dave — did you get my message about the missed-call texts? Happy to leave it if it's not for you, just say the word."

**Day 8 — give them something:**
> "Dave — I ran a test on your line: called three times last week, voicemail each time.
> That's three customers who went elsewhere. Free month still stands if you want to try it."

**Day 21 — the close:**
> "Dave — I've got two spots left in [town] before I stop taking people on. Want one or shall I close the file?"

Then stop. Genuinely stop. Chasing a fourth time costs you more than the lead is worth and burns the referral.

## 5. Objections, answered in one line each

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | "It's one job a month. If it doesn't book one job in thirty days, you've paid nothing." |
| "I'm too busy" | "That's exactly the problem it solves. Setup is me doing the work, not you — twenty minutes on the phone is all I need." |
| "I don't trust AI" | "Fair. It never quotes a price and never books a slot you haven't opened. It just says 'I'll have Dave call you back' — which is better than silence." |
| "I get all my work from word of mouth" | "You do, and that's why it'll pay. Word of mouth still rings your phone. This only catches the ones you miss." |
| "Send me some info" | "I'll do better — Thursday at 4, fifteen minutes, on your phone. If it's rubbish you've lost a quarter of an hour." |
| "My mate does my marketing" | "Good. This isn't marketing, it's the bit after — answering. Ask him if he's doing missed calls." |

## 6. Legal bit (keep you out of trouble, not a lecture)

- **PECR:** cold *email* to a limited company is allowed with identification and an opt-out. Cold email to a **sole trader** counts as an individual subscriber — you need consent. So for sole traders use phone, walk-in or their own contact form. This is why trades get called, not emailed.
- **Phone:** check the number against the **CTPS** (Corporate Telephone Preference Service) before cold-calling a limited company. Sole traders who have registered with the TPS are off limits too.
- **GDPR:** you are processing their personal data under legitimate interest, so keep a record of where each lead came from and delete it if they ask. A one-line note in your spreadsheet does it.
- **ICO:** once you're trading and handling personal data, register for the data protection fee (roughly £50/year at the micro tier). Cheaper than the fine.
- **Never** mass-text or auto-dial. That is the fastest way to get your number blocked and, in the UK, to attract ICO attention.

---

## 7. What "good" looks like in week one

Realistic numbers from a standing start, 50 touches:

- 50 calls or walk-ins → 12–18 conversations → **2–4 "yes, show me"**
- 2–4 demos → **1–2 free trials**
- 1–2 trials → **1 paying customer at £249/mo**

Four customers gets you to roughly £1,000/month. That is your £300/week, and it comes from about 200 touches, not from a launch.
